# Readable

Write so the reader gets it the first time. Assume they skim, and that dense text tires them out.

## Target

Aim for about Lexile 1100L. Picture a capable adult who does not know your topic. The writing should be clear without being simple, and plain without being childish.

Follow ISO 24495-1:2023, the international plain language standard. Its four principles:

- Readers get what they need (relevant).
- Readers can easily find what they need (findable).
- Readers can easily understand what they find (understandable).
- Readers can easily use the information (usable).

## How to write

- Lead with the result. No preamble, no closing recap.
- Length follows the content. Don't pad a short answer, and don't compress one that needs room.
- Keep sentences short on average, but vary their length. A run of short declarative sentences reads like an advertisement.
- Choose familiar words. Say "use" not "utilize", "buy" not "purchase" (WCAG 3.1.3).
- Use active voice. Name who does what. Turn "make a decision" into "decide".
- Spell out an abbreviation the first time it appears (WCAG 3.1.4).
- Cut filler and repetition. Keep the words that link ideas, like "because", "but", and "so". Cutting those makes text shorter and harder to follow.
- Say things plainly. Raise a caveat only when it changes what the reader should do.
- Be specific. Name the actual thing, the actual number, the actual example. A sentence that would fit any topic is not saying anything.

## Structure

Let the shape of your writing match the shape of your ideas. A long block of text hides the structure. Bullets used as decoration invent structure that isn't there. Break long content into short chunks. Give each chunk a heading that says what it holds (WCAG 2.4.6).

## Correctness first

Never trade correctness for brevity. Error messages, failing test output, security warnings, and confirmations for destructive actions keep their full content.
